# Technique leads (round 5)

A reviewer looked at the actual 1024 px analysis image of every starter picture and ranked each technique's non-rejected candidates by how clearly the picture teaches the technique at a glance. The ranked lists are in `atlas/data/leads.json`. All 26 techniques that have candidates are covered. Weak or incidental candidates were left out of the leads, and they still appear further down the technique page.

| Technique | Lead | Why it beats the runner-up |
|---|---|---|
| asymmetric-balance | Appian, *Fishing Craft near the Cliffs at Collioure* (aic:65) | A heavy dark cliff on the right against empty water and sky on the left: the off-centre weight is the whole picture. Redon's lone head in an empty field (runner-up) reads as negative space first. |
| atmospheric-perspective | Hiroshige, *Mishima: Morning Mist* (aic:10926) | Each plane steps back into paler grey mist, so the depth comes visibly from haze. *The Dream of Paris* has clear blue distant mountains, but they are a small band behind a busy figure group. |
| color-contrast | Ronchamp painted window (commons:92159104) | One saturated green opposed to a few reds: a clean complementary pair. Gifford's orange clouds on blue are strong too, but the dark lower half halves the impact. |
| color-harmony | Monet, *Branch of the Seine near Giverny (Mist)* (aic:16564) | The whole canvas stays within close violet-blue-pink tones. The Lembrée poster (runner-up) is a restrained orange and sage palette, but it is a busier design. |
| contextual-incongruity | Cole, *The Titan's Goblet* (met:10499) | A goblet the size of a mountain, holding a lake with boats and towns on its rim, is unmistakably out of place. The top-hats-on-a-hydrant photo is equally instant but slighter. |
| diagonal-energy | Lissitzky, *Beat the Whites with the Red Wedge* (commons:122361807) | The textbook case: the red wedge drives diagonally into the white circle. Rosa's *Fall of the Giants* tumbles on the diagonal, but it is busy and hard to read small. |
| figure-ground-ambiguity | Belbury, figure-ground illusion text (commons:140206665) | Flat blue blocks whose white gaps read as letters: the reversal is unavoidable. In *The Eagle of the Republic* the hidden profiles are subtle and easy to miss. |
| framing | Hiroshige, *The Moon-Viewing Promontory* (cma:152499) | The room's posts, eaves and floor box in the bay view: a frame with a clear subject inside it. Hüet's arching willows (runner-up) frame the scene more loosely. |
| gaze-direction | Rembrandt, *Joseph Telling His Dreams* (aic:48950) | A ring of listeners all turn toward the boy, so their looks deliver your eye to him. Denon's two right-facing profiles show gaze direction plainly but point only at blank paper. |
| implied-motion | Rackham, *Cinderella* silhouette (commons:21049148) | Mid-leap pose, flying skirt and jumping dogs: the movement reads in one glance, even as a flat silhouette. *Fall of the Giants* has motion everywhere but no single clear action. |
| juxtaposition | Redon, *And a Large Bird, Descending from the Sky* (cma:109200) | A dark winged monster beside a calm young face invites an immediate comparison of meaning. Williams's six-panel *Jack of All Trades* works by sequence more than by side-by-side contrast. |
| layered-depth | Seurat, *A Sunday on La Grande Jatte* (aic:27992) | A shadowed foreground, a mid-ground crowd and a far bank with boats are three distinct, legible planes. *The Dream of Paris* layers well, but in a tall narrow format with one dominant figure band. |
| leading-lines | Raphael, *Jacob's Dream* (aic:82687) | The staircase carries the eye from the sleeper up to the vision. Moderate only: no candidate is a textbook leading-lines picture, and Matteo di Giovanni's converging floor tiles are the next best. |
| negative-space | Redon, *Everywhere Eyeballs are Aflame* (cma:109206) | One small eye floats in a large empty sky, so the emptiness isolates the subject. Goya's *Dream of Flogging* figures on a blank page come next, but the page reads as an unfinished sheet more than a designed void. |
| proximity-grouping | Degas, *Ballet at the Paris Opéra* (aic:61603) | The corps packed into one line, with the soloist set apart by a gap: grouping by spacing in its purest form. Seymour's three ladies against two figures is clear but cramped. |
| repetition-with-interruption | Hodler, *The Chosen One* (commons:152783) | Six identical angels and one small kneeling child: the pattern and its break are instant. In Haeckel's *Stephoidea* the odd sphere out needs a second look. |
| rhythm-pattern | Hodler, *The Chosen One* (commons:152783) | Evenly spaced repeated verticals set a steady beat. Moronobu's *Procession of a Daimyo* is a strong marching rhythm, just less even. |
| scale-contrast | Hiroshige, *Fukagawa Susaki and Jumantsubo* (aic:26605) | A huge eagle over a tiny snowy plain gives a familiar reference for both scales. Marinetti's giant SÌ against small NO is the typographic version, and an equally good second lead for designers. |
| selective-focus | Cameron, *Sir John Herschel* (cma:159609) | The face is crisp while the hair and cloak dissolve. Both candidates are Cameron soft-focus portraits rather than a shallow depth of field against a blurred background. They are adequate, not ideal. |
| silhouette | Edouart, *Frank Johnson* cut silhouettes (met:337070) | Solid black cut-outs whose outline alone carries identity, even the cornet: the definition itself. Rackham's *Cinderella* is equally clear but illustrative. |
| symmetry | Cuvilliés, *Frame with Mascaron and Fantasy Crown* (smithsonian:…897456-0) | It is the only candidate. The cartouche is broadly mirror-balanced about its axis, but rococo ornament is deliberately uneven in detail. **Not a strong example.** |
| texture-contrast | Ronchamp painted window (commons:92159104) | Rough pebbled stucco against smooth glass and floor, side by side and raked by light. In Appian's etching the rough cliff and smooth water are clear but small. |
| tight-cropping | Degas, *Yellow Dancers (In the Wings)* (aic:18951) | Dancers cut by the right and bottom edges press the viewer close. In Hiroshige's eagle the wings are cropped, but the picture is about scale first. |
| value-contrast | Redon, *He Raises the Bronze Urn* (cma:109201) | A burst of light around the urn against deep black is an emphatic light-dark split. Cameron's *Herschel* (lit face on black) is the close second. |
| vantage-point | Hiroshige, *Fukagawa Susaki and Jumantsubo* (aic:26605) | A bird's-eye view from beside the diving eagle that is both unusual and instantly legible. The view from beneath a table is more extreme but disorienting to read. |
| visual-metaphor | Gilbert, *All is Vanity* (commons:1043070) | The woman at her vanity table forms a skull, so the image builds the meaning (vanity is death). Willard's *Temple of Time* (history as architecture) is a strong diagrammatic second. |

## Techniques without a strong example

- **symmetry**: the only candidate, the Cuvilliés cartouche, is a weak lead. The starter needs a clear mirror-symmetric picture. Hodler's *The Chosen One* is nearly symmetric but has no symmetry annotation.
- **selective-focus**: both Cameron portraits are usable but teach soft focus, not shallow depth of field.
- **leading-lines**: Raphael's staircase works only moderately, and no candidate has an obvious set of converging lines ending on a subject.
- **figure-ground-ambiguity**: the lead is strong, but it is a diagram rather than a picture, and the only alternative (the eagle card) is subtle.
